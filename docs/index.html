1 SEO and content teams cannot manually inspect every page in a large inventory, creating a need for systematic prioritization.
2 This study builds a decision-support system on 331,437 aggregated pages across 55 clients, using two March 2026 search-visibility features: impression volume and average search position.

02 — Introduction

The Decision Problem

Decision
Which pages should a human SEO reviewer inspect first?
Unit
Page × client.
Output
Priority score / ranked queue.
Metric
Precision@K.

A portfolio of hundreds of thousands of pages cannot be reviewed at uniform depth. This is a triage problem: given limited review capacity, which pages deserve human inspection first? A ranked review queue scored by a model identifies where human review is most likely to be productive — not an automation problem, since editorial judgment remains essential.

DOES

  • Rank pages by observed search-visibility patterns
  • Produce a prioritized queue for human inspection
  • Provide decision-support with reason codes

DOES NOT

  • Prove causal impact of content changes
  • Predict Google's algorithm or ranking factors
  • Guarantee content-refresh success

LR and baseline top-1000 queues share only 6 pages (0.6%). The two methods identify fundamentally different pages as highest priority.

06 — Limitations

What This Study Does Not Claim

  1. Proxy & Label — The target (opportunity_proxy) is a decision-support proxy, not a measure of actual business impact. impressions > 0 appears in both the feature set and the label definition, creating a structural relationship. Precision@K is partly influenced by this overlap. This is not a clean future prediction target.
  2. Generalization — GroupKFold with 55 clients tests across groups within one portfolio; results may not generalize to different industries or search landscapes. One client dominates ~50% of the top-1000 queue. Random Forest was evaluated in Week 5 but not independently audited in Week 6 and not used for the Week 7 action queue.
  3. Observational — All associations are observed in search data only. No causality is established. The output is a ranked review queue, not prescribed actions; human judgment remains essential for every editorial decision.
  4. Production Scope — The queue prioritizes review; it does not automate publishing decisions. Only March 2026 data — no seasonal, trend, or longitudinal patterns captured. Results are directional evidence for similar triage tasks, not general-purpose SEO prediction.

Recommendations

  1. 01
    Start with the top-ranked pages The 1,000-page REVIEW tier. ~971 proxy-positive — pages with impressions but no clicks.
    Signal: impressions present, no clicks → Action: content/metadata review → Rationale: highest probability of productive human intervention
  2. 02
    Use reason codes and archetypes Each page has a reason code and behavioral archetype providing starting context for the human reviewer.
    Signal: position + impression combination → Action: archetype-guided investigation → Rationale: different patterns require different review approaches
  3. 03
    Monitor the middle tier 174,304 MONITOR pages have observable signal. Track for changes that might elevate priority.
    Signal: impressions > 0 but below REVIEW threshold → Action: periodic reassessment → Rationale: position shifts may surface new opportunities
  4. 04
    Maintain human judgment The model scores where to look; the human decides what to do. Publishing, rewriting, merging, pruning all require editorial judgment.
    Signal: model output is a rank, not a decision → Action: human review as final step → Rationale: editorial context cannot be automated
